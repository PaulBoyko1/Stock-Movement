# Evidence register: 27 research papers

Research date: September 15, 2026. This is a curated first-pass literature screen, not an exhaustive survey. Titles, publication identity and the findings below were checked against primary author, institutional or publisher material. Access varies: some claims are verified from abstracts, others from full-paper sections. None has been independently replicated in Stock-Movement.

“Supported” here always refers to a bounded historical finding. Implementation priorities and proposed adaptations are our research judgments. Contemporary liquid U.S. stocks may behave differently from the original samples.

## Medium and longer horizons

**R01 — Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen (2013), Value and Momentum Everywhere, Journal of Finance.** [Author research page](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Value-and-Momentum-Everywhere); [publisher](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.12021). Primary abstract/summary screened. Evidence across eight markets/asset classes supports researching value and momentum jointly, including their diversification. This does not establish short-horizon stock timing or an immediately executable long-only strategy. Application: establish separate momentum and valuation baselines before combining them.

**R02 — Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen (2012), Time Series Momentum, Journal of Financial Economics.** [Author research page](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Time-Series-Momentum). Primary summary screened. Past own returns forecast returns across 58 futures/forward contracts in their sample. This is different from ranking individual stocks against peers; transferring the result to sector ETFs is an adaptation requiring testing. Application: simple own-trend benchmarks and risk context.

**R03 — Novy-Marx (2013), The Other Side of Value: The Gross Profitability Premium, Journal of Financial Economics.** [NBER working version and publication record](https://www.nber.org/papers/w15940). Primary abstract screened; working version dates to 2010 and has a slightly longer title. Gross profits relative to assets help explain cross-sectional average returns and complement value. Application: a sector-aware profitability baseline. Gross profitability is not synonymous with every quality metric or a forecast of the next earnings-day move.

**R04 — Fama & French (2015), A Five-Factor Asset Pricing Model, Journal of Financial Economics.** [Publisher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X14002323). Abstract and available sections screened. Size, value, profitability and investment factors improve explanation of average returns relative to their three-factor model, with documented failures. Application: benchmark risk exposures and test whether a proposed signal adds anything beyond known factors. An explanatory asset-pricing model is not an automatic stock-picking system.

**R05 — Daniel & Moskowitz (2016), Momentum Crashes, Journal of Financial Economics.** [NBER record and working version](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20439). Primary abstract screened; working version dates to 2014. Momentum can suffer severe losses in rebound episodes following market declines and elevated volatility. Application: expose tail risk and evaluate regime robustness. Do not infer that a particular volatility filter can reliably avoid future crashes without testing it.

**R06 — Frazzini & Pedersen (2014), Betting Against Beta, Journal of Financial Economics.** [Author research page](https://www.aqr.com/insights/research/journal-article/betting-against-beta). Primary summary screened. Evidence across countries/assets supports a low-beta versus high-beta premium in the studied portfolio constructions. Application: risk-aware comparisons. The academic leveraged long-short factor is materially different from simply buying a low-volatility ETF.

**R07 — Gu, Kelly & Xiu (2020), Empirical Asset Pricing via Machine Learning, Review of Financial Studies.** [NBER paper and publication record](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25398). Primary abstract screened; working version 2018, revised 2019. Nonlinear methods capture useful interactions in asset-risk-premium forecasts, with momentum, liquidity and volatility prominent. Application: challenger models after linear baselines. The results do not establish minute-level forecasts or justify using an LLM as a calibrated return predictor.

**R08 — Loughran & McDonald (2011), When Is a Liability Not a Liability? Textual Analysis, Dictionaries, and 10-Ks, Journal of Finance.** [Publisher](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01625.x). Primary abstract screened. General-purpose negative-word dictionaries misclassify financial language; domain-specific measures behave differently. Application: source-linked document changes and financial-language baselines. Sentiment measurement is not proof of future net returns.

## Earnings and industry information

**R09 — Bernard & Thomas (1990), Evidence That Stock Prices Do Not Fully Reflect the Implications of Current Earnings for Future Earnings, Journal of Accounting and Economics.** [University-hosted paper](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/bitstream/handle/2027.42/28288/0000041.pdf). Paper abstract/relevant material screened. Historical evidence links earnings persistence with subsequent announcement reactions. Application: test earnings surprise and persistence jointly. Modern liquid-universe profitability remains unestablished.

**R10 — Yuan Zhang (2008), Analyst Responsiveness and the Post-Earnings-Announcement Drift, Journal of Accounting and Economics.** [Publisher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0165410108000220); [author paper](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/3737/zhang.pdf). Abstract and relevant sections screened. Prompt forecast revisions coincide with faster initial incorporation and less later drift. Application: measure revision timing, breadth and magnitude. More rapid positive revisions need not mean more remaining upside.

**R11 — Chordia, Goyal, Gil Sadka, Ronnie Sadka & Shivakumar (2009), Liquidity and the Post-Earnings-Announcement Drift, Financial Analysts Journal.** [CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2009/liquidity-and-the-post-earnings-announcement-drift); [publisher](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.2469/faj.v65.n4.3). Primary summary/abstract screened. Monthly value-weighted long-short drift was 0.04% in the most-liquid group versus 2.43% in the least-liquid; estimated costs absorbed 70–100% of paper profits. This is a critical constraint for our agreed liquid universe, not a current return estimate.

**R12 — Moskowitz & Grinblatt (1999), Do Industries Explain Momentum?, Journal of Finance.** [Publisher](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/0022-1082.00146). Primary abstract screened. Industry momentum accounts for a substantial portion of stock momentum in the historical sample. Application: display stock strength alongside industry and market. It does not verify a fixed sector-cycle clock, RRG quadrant strategy, or any particular ETF allocation rule.

**R13 — Hsu & Wang (2021), Bundled Earnings Guidance and Analysts' Forecast Revisions, Contemporary Accounting Research.** [Publisher](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1911-3846.12686). Open article/relevant sections screened. Analysts respond differently when guidance accompanies earnings, motivating separate records for actual results, guidance and analyst updates. Residual confounding remains possible. Application: test information timing; market response does not itself imply post-release tradable alpha.

## Reliability, replication and selection bias

**R14 — Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016), … and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns, Review of Financial Studies.** [Author-hosted published paper](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P118_and_the_cross.PDF). Abstract/relevant sections screened. Extensive factor search invalidates casual reliance on conventional significance thresholds; the paper argues for stronger evidence for new factors. Application: register all experiments and control multiplicity. Its suggested t-statistic above 3 is not a universal certification rule.

**R15 — Hou, Xue & Zhang (2020), Replicating Anomalies, Review of Financial Studies.** [Final publisher article](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/33/5/2019/5236964). Final abstract/results screened. Under their NYSE breakpoints and value weighting, 65% of 452 anomalies fail ordinary significance and 82% fail their multiple-testing hurdle. Use the final 452-anomaly figures, not older working-paper counts. Application: test liquidity and weighting sensitivity.

**R16 — Chen & Zimmermann (2022), Open Source Cross-Sectional Asset Pricing, Critical Finance Review.** [Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1561/104.00000112); [authors' code repository](https://github.com/OpenSourceAP/CrossSection/). Abstract and code-project documentation screened. Under original-like replication procedures, 98% of 161 originally clear predictors reproduce t-statistics above 1.96. This is an essential counterpoint: historical reproducibility, robustness to altered construction, and modern net tradability are different questions. Application: use published code as a replication starting point.

**R17 — McLean & Pontiff (2016), Does Academic Research Destroy Stock Return Predictability?, Journal of Finance.** [Publisher](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12365). Primary abstract screened. For 97 predictors, returns fell on average 26% out of sample and 58% after publication relative to original in-sample performance. Application: show post-publication and recent results separately. These averages are not mechanical haircuts for every strategy.

**R18 — Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu (2017), The Probability of Backtest Overfitting, Journal of Computational Finance.** [University record](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/4w1110bb); [author manuscript](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf). Abstract/relevant methodology screened. Selecting the best historical variant creates selection risk. Application: preserve the entire search history and evaluate overfitting diagnostics. Those diagnostics supplement chronological tests, realistic costs and forward monitoring.

## Intraday, price patterns and microstructure

**R19 — Lo, Mamaysky & Wang (2000), Foundations of Technical Analysis: Computational Algorithms, Statistical Inference, and Empirical Implementation, Journal of Finance.** [NBER paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7613.pdf); [publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00265). Paper methods/results screened. Some automatically detected patterns change conditional return distributions in 1962–1996 U.S. data. Application: mechanically defined patterns as challenger features. Informational content is not proof of modern net profitability, and a turning point cannot be used before it is confirmable.

**R20 — Heston, Korajczyk & Sadka (2010), Intraday Patterns in the Cross-section of Stock Returns, Journal of Finance.** [Author-hosted paper](https://www.bauer.uh.edu/departments/finance/documents/Heston_Korajczyk_Sadka_paper_UH.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01573.x). Methods and cost discussion screened. Same-time-of-day continuation and short reversal coexist. Buying at ask and selling at bid produced negative returns across examined groups/times. Application: execution scheduling may be more useful than adding round-trip trades.

**R21 — Gao, Han, Li & Zhou (2018), Market Intraday Momentum, Journal of Financial Economics.** [Publisher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X18301351). Abstract and available paper material screened. In their S&P 500 ETF sample, the previous-close-to-10:00 ET return predicts the final half-hour; the predictor includes overnight movement. The study reports out-of-sample evidence and viability under its cost assumptions. Application: precise late-session baseline requiring current replication and executable-price costs.

**R22 — Baltussen, Da, Lammers & Martens (2021), Hedging Demand and Market Intraday Momentum, Journal of Financial Economics.** [Author-hosted published paper](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf). Methods/results and caveats screened. Broad futures evidence links the return before the final half-hour to that interval and subsequent reversal. Main strategy results exclude costs. Application: compare opening-only and later-day predictors; an inferred hedging-demand mechanism is not observed dealer inventory.

**R23 — Cont, Kukanov & Stoikov (2014), The Price Impact of Order Book Events, Journal of Financial Econometrics.** [Author preprint/publication metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.6402); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/jjfinec/nbt003). Primary paper summary/methodology screened. Order-flow imbalance relates to contemporaneous short-interval price changes, with depth affecting sensitivity. Application: execution diagnostics and lagged forecasts. Explaining a move with events from that same interval is not forecasting the next interval.

**R24 — Gould & Bonart (2016), Queue Imbalance as a One-Tick-Ahead Price Predictor in a Limit Order Book, Market Microstructure and Liquidity.** [2015 author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03492); [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1142/S2382626616500064). Full-method sections screened. Queue imbalance predicts next mid-price direction, particularly for large-tick stocks. The random split and sampling choices need replacement with causal sampling and chronological holdouts for deployment. Directional accuracy does not establish profitable fills.

**R25 — Hendershott & Menkveld (2014), Price Pressures, Journal of Financial Economics.** [Author-hosted published paper](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/hender/price_pressures.pdf). Relevant methods/results screened. Historical intermediary inventory and a structural model explain temporary price pressure. Proprietary inputs and future-informed Kalman smoothing prevent direct use as a live public signal. Application: motivate reversal tests; use causal filtering if adapting the model.

**R26 — Sullivan, Timmermann & White (1999), Data-Snooping, Technical Trading Rule Performance, and the Bootstrap, Journal of Finance.** [Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00163); [published paper copy](https://www.kevinsheppard.com/files/teaching/mfe/advanced-econometrics/Sullivan_Timmermann_White.pdf). Relevant methodology/results screened. Tests 7,846 technical rules with a Reality Check; early evidence differs from later-period evidence. Application: assess the whole searched family rather than celebrate its best member. The conclusion is more nuanced than “all technical analysis fails.”

**R27 — Bajgrowicz & Scaillet (2012), Technical Trading Revisited: False Discoveries, Persistence Tests, and Transaction Costs, Journal of Financial Economics.** [University repository](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:79889); [publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2012.06.001). Accepted manuscript methodology/results screened. Selection timing, false discoveries and transaction costs undermine apparent profits in a large technical-rule family. Application: choose models on net development-period performance and keep a genuinely subsequent evaluation period.

## Data and visualization references (not additional alpha papers)

- [Kenneth French Data Library](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/data_library.html): factor/industry baseline data; archive vintages and construction notes.
- [French daily momentum construction](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/Data_Library/det_mom_factor_daily.html): explicitly documents prior 2–12 returns; adapted liquid-universe constructions must be labeled adaptations.
- [SEC EDGAR API documentation](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces): submissions and XBRL facts; original filing availability and metric context still matter.
- [Original RRG user guide](https://relativerotationgraphs.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Relative-Rotation-Graph-Thomson-Reuters-Guide.pdf): proprietary JdK indicators, non-guaranteed rotation, and relative-versus-absolute performance. Use a disclosed alternative or separately evaluate licensing.

## What is not yet established

None of these sources validates our proposed signal combinations, exact UI score, data vendor, current universe thresholds, rotation formula, execution rules, or automated strategy. Unverified social-media strategies and attractive historical charts are not admitted as evidence. New research must document sample, market, publication version, data availability, baseline, transaction costs, out-of-sample design, and reproducibility.
